**1.**
- **What happened:** Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa called for extending the existing de facto data center moratorium until the legislature can pass rules. Her proposed priorities include giving local communities the right to vote on data center projects and requiring companies to prove households won't pay more for electricity or water.
- **Why it matters to Crusoe:** Crusoe's Abilene campuses for Oracle and Microsoft and its two other Texas campuses could face permit delays or new conditions, such as local votes, union-job rules and proof of no ratepayer impact, if the pause on new state environmental permits continues and her legislation passes. The next markers are whether the PUCT/ERCOT audit ends in December as ERCOT expects, whether Abbott lifts the pause, and whether the legislature takes up local-vote rights.
- **Business impact:**
  - **Direction:** Risk
  - **Channel:** Siting & timeline
  - **Size:** unsized
  - **Consider:** Which Texas expansion phases at Abilene or the other two Texas campuses still need state environmental permits that the current halt would block?
- **Confidence:** Confirmed

**2.**
- **What happened:** Crusoe unveiled Spark Liquid, a sixth-generation modular data center unit built for Nvidia GB300 NVL72 and Vera Rubin NVL72 racks. Production will join the existing Spark line in Brighton, Colorado, in 2027.
- **Why it matters to Crusoe:** The Brighton, CO Spark factory gains a liquid-cooled product aligned with Nvidia's DSX POD. It could let Crusoe Cloud add capacity in smaller power blocks while large grid interconnects and permits are stalled. Watch whether 2027 production starts on schedule and whether Spark Liquid deployments come online in the few months from a ready site that Crusoe claims.
- **Business impact:**
  - **Direction:** Opportunity
  - **Channel:** Customers & pipeline
  - **Size:** 3.5 MW (stated in the source)

- **Confidence:** Confirmed
- **Source:** [Modular AI Data Centers Gain Ground as Crusoe Debuts Unit](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/modular-data-centers/modular-ai-data-centers-gain-ground-as-crusoe-debuts-unit) (Data Center Knowledge)
- <sub>**Evidence:** "said its new Spark Liquid unit is designed to support up to 3.5 MW of compute capacity" (article text) · **Size:** "Crusoe's 3.5 MW Spark Liquid unit highlights how factory-built, modular data centers are becoming a faster path to AI capacity" (article text)</sub>

**3.**
- **What happened:** OpenAI published a Compute Campus Policy and a National Community Compact for sites it owns and operates. It pledged to drop NDAs with public officials, publish site-specific impact plans, smooth grid load, supply power back to the grid, and insure against stranded assets.
- **Why it matters to Crusoe:** These pledges could become the baseline that utilities and regulators expect from other large AI campuses, including Crusoe's power arrangements in Abilene and Missouri. That could add demand-response, grid-support or disclosure obligations that raise costs. The link is indirect because the policy covers only OpenAI-owned sites; it would tighten if regulators or hyperscaler customers begin to require similar terms from developers.
- **Business impact:**
  - **Direction:** Risk
  - **Channel:** Costs & margins
  - **Size:** unsized
  - **Consider:** Whether Crusoe's on-site generation at its campuses could be offered as grid-support or load-smoothing capacity, matching OpenAI's commitments before regulators require it.
- **Confidence:** Confirmed
- **Source:** [OpenAI promises to drop NDAs and increase community transparency, following anti-data center wave](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/openai-promises-to-drop-ndas-and-increase-community-transparency-following-anti-data-center-wave/) (Data Center Dynamics)
- <sub>**Evidence:** "OpenAI said that it will no longer use non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) with public officials" (article text)</sub>
